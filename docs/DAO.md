# DAO

This document explains why MetaFor needs a [DAO](https://ethereum.org/dao/) layer and how that layer is meant to work conceptually.
It is not a smart-contract specification and not an implementation document.

## Why MetaFor Needs DAO

MetaFor is conceived not as a closed product owned by one platform, but as a common space uniting our universe and digital reality.
If that space is meant to include people, devices, the applications running on those devices, agents, [AGI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_general_intelligence), and shared structure, then admission cannot remain a purely personal or purely corporate matter forever.
For MetaFor, this is not a secondary political theme.
When AGI becomes real, ownership and governance belong to its final path rather than to an optional layer around it.

DAO appears here not as a fashionable token wrapper, but as a governance mechanism for a common space:

- who may participate in shaping the shared space,
- which meta-entities may enter it,
- under what rights and restrictions they may exist there,
- how harmful, incompatible, or disputed entities may be limited or removed,
- how inheritance and continuity of accepted meta are handled collectively rather than arbitrarily.

In short:
the DAO is needed because MetaFor is aimed at becoming a common space, and a common space requires a formal collective admission layer.

## Why This Matters Materially

Without such a layer, people risk being displaced from work and left without a livelihood while stronger intelligent systems accumulate under states, corporations, and private platforms.

MetaFor takes the opposite direction.
People should not remain outside the system that replaces them.
They should have a way to participate inside it: in the construction, admission, inheritance, ownership, and governance of intelligent entities.

The point of DAO here is therefore not symbolic voting for its own sake.
It is a way to preserve human agency, economic continuation, and collective legitimacy inside the AGI environment itself.

## Why TON

The DAO concept for MetaFor is tied to [TON](https://ton.org/en/) as the long-term governance substrate because TON is the closest fit to MetaFor both architecturally and ideologically.
The purpose of TON here is not branding.
It is a public, verifiable, shared layer whose native parallelism, speed, and operational cost fit the direction of MetaFor much better than a slow, heavy, or purely symbolic governance shell.

But the strongest reason is deeper than performance alone:
the model of MetaFor itself maps onto TON unusually well.
For MetaFor, TON is not just a convenient blockchain.
It is the clearest long-term substrate for a space where many entities, permissions, inheritances, and collective decisions must coexist without collapsing into one private center.

In that model, TON is the place where the fact of collective permission is anchored.
MetaFor itself remains the architectural environment in which the accepted structures live and act.

## What the DAO Governs

The DAO does not redefine the ontology of MetaFor.
It does not replace `Dark`, `Boundary`, `Bulk`, or the role of `meta`.

Instead, it governs access to the shared space built on top of that ontology.
Its direct object is `meta` as the formal source of structure, connectivity, behavior, and manifestation.

Conceptually, the DAO governs:

- admission of new meta,
- activation of a specific accepted version,
- restriction of risky or disputed meta,
- exclusion of inadmissible meta,
- inheritance relations between meta,
- rights and limitations of accepted meta within the shared space,
- evolution of the collective rules themselves.

## Meta as Inheritable meta-NFT

In this governance model, meta is treated not as a decorative description but as a formal entity that can be owned, addressed, versioned, inherited, and admitted into the common space.

That leads to a two-layer reading:

- meta as the architectural description of structure, behavior, state, connectivity, and manifestation,
- meta as NFT-like digital ownership and continuity unit, so that contribution to the shared space can remain attributable and inheritable.

This is why the document speaks about **meta-NFT**.
The point is not collectible imagery.
The point is that a meta-entity can become a durable, inherited, governable object in the ecosystem.
In practical terms, such entities may carry skills, items, behaviors, tools, interfaces, and other meaningful structures of the shared space.

## Contribution and Economic Continuity

If meta can be owned, admitted, inherited, and circulated, then contributors can preserve more than authorship.
They can preserve an economic position inside the space they help to build.

The intended direction is that contributors should be able to receive value from the sale and circulation of meta-NFT rather than watching all value settle only at the platform layer.
That matters especially when those entities carry skills, items, behaviors, tools, interfaces, and other inherited structures that remain useful inside the environment.

This is not the same as claiming that the final economic policy is already defined.
It is a statement of architectural intent:
useful inherited structures should remain attributable, ownable, and economically connected to the people who created them.

## The Central Rule

The central DAO rule for MetaFor is simple:

> A meta-entity may exist as an owned NFT-like object, but it becomes part of the common MetaFor space only if the collective registry admits it.

This means ownership alone is not enough.
A person may create, hold, and publish a meta-entity, but the right for that entity to participate in the common space appears only after collective admission.

## Registry as the Source of Permission

The DAO therefore needs a formal registry of admitted meta.
Conceptually, that registry is the single source of truth for collective permission.

The registry answers questions such as:

- is this meta only a candidate,
- is it accepted,
- is it active,
- is it restricted,
- is it excluded,
- which version is currently active,
- from which accepted parents does it inherit,
- which rights and limitations apply to it.

Without registry admission, a meta-entity may still exist as an authored object, but it does not belong to the common space in the full sense.

## Participants and Collective Will

The DAO layer also defines who has the right to participate in governance.
For MetaFor, the important principle is that collective will should not collapse into simple capital purchase.

So the intended direction is:

- participation should be based on recognized membership,
- economic stake may create responsibility and cost for abuse,
- but money alone should not automatically equal architectural control over the shared space.

That principle matters because MetaFor is not only an economy of digital objects.
It is also a governance problem of a shared world.

## Lifecycle of Meta Admission

At the conceptual level, the lifecycle of a meta-entity in the DAO looks like this:

1. A meta-entity is created and published by its author.
2. It is proposed for inclusion in the common space.
3. The community reviews its meaning, compatibility, safety, and inheritance relations.
4. A collective decision is made.
5. If accepted, the registry grants it an admitted status and defines its rights or limits.
6. If needed, later proposals may update, restrict, archive, or exclude it.

The important point is not the exact voting UI or contract format.
The important point is that admission becomes procedural, legible, and collectively anchored.

## Inheritance

Inheritance is a key reason the DAO cannot be reduced to a simple NFT ownership list.
In MetaFor, meta is not flat content.
It may define structure that later becomes the parent of derived entities.

That means governance must care not only about an isolated meta-object, but also about:

- what it inherits from,
- what may inherit from it,
- what happens to descendants if a parent becomes disputed or excluded.

In practical governance terms, inheritance must be explicit, traceable, and subject to collective rules.
Otherwise harmful or incompatible structures could spread through the system under the surface of derivation.

## Restriction and Exclusion

The DAO is needed not only to say "yes".
It is also needed to say "not yet", "only with limits", or "no".

That implies several conceptual states:

- `candidate` for something submitted but not yet admitted,
- `accepted` for something admitted in principle,
- `active` for something currently allowed to operate in the shared space,
- `restricted` for something limited to review, sandboxing, or constrained use,
- `excluded` for something no longer admissible,
- `archived` for something preserved historically but not active.

These states let MetaFor remain open without becoming structurally defenseless.

## What the DAO Adds to MetaFor

If this layer is realized well, the DAO adds several things at once:

- collective admission into the shared space instead of arbitrary platform permission,
- continuity of authorship and ownership for contributed meta,
- a material participation path so contributors can retain economic position through the sale and circulation of admitted meta-NFT,
- governance of inheritance, not only governance of isolated objects,
- a way to keep the space open without making it unbounded,
- a bridge between architectural meaning and collective legitimacy.

In that sense, the DAO is not external to MetaFor.
It is one of the long-term conditions for MetaFor to become a genuine common environment rather than a private system with open-source code.
It is also part of how MetaFor aims to ensure that people are not reduced to passive users of AGI controlled from above, but remain participants with ownership, agency, and means of continuation inside the space itself.

## What This Document Does Not Claim

This document does not claim that the DAO layer is already fully implemented.
It does not define the final contract system, the final voting formula, or the final economic policy.

It only fixes the intended role of DAO in MetaFor:
as the collective admission system of inheritable meta-NFT into the common space, anchored through TON and governed by formal shared decisions rather than private discretion alone.
